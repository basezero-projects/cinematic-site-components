Module 36 - Curved Path Motion

Let the offer travel through the proof.

Use a path when a page needs to connect scattered proof points into one clear journey. The visitor sees where the story starts, what it passes through, and where it lands.

Route the story.

A product, quote, or case study moves through the proof instead of appearing as another static card.

Brief

Set the job.

Start with the customer problem, campaign idea, or service request so the motion has a reason to move.

Proof

Pass through evidence.

Let testimonials, work samples, locations, specs, or results become stops on the route.

Offer

Arrive at the pitch.

The destination should feel earned because the visitor watched the support build around it.

Action

Make the next step clear.

End with one concrete action: schedule, quote, reserve, buy, or open the case study.

Responsive SVG path

One moving object, four content beats.

The path draws as the object travels. Active cards brighten only when the moving object reaches their area, which keeps the section readable instead of turning it into decoration.

Best for

Service journeys, product launch flows, case-study timelines, quote funnels, fulfillment routes, and any page where the visitor needs to understand sequence.

Avoid

Pages with no real sequence, dense body copy, or effects where every card is equally important at the same time.

Why this belongs in the library.

A curved path is more useful than another reveal when a page has scattered proof. It creates spatial continuity: the visitor can feel a quote becoming a plan, a plan becoming proof, and proof becoming the CTA. Keep the object meaningful, keep the path short, and make sure the final stop has a real action.
